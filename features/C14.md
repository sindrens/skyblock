# Season economy curve · id: C14 · status: Draft · version: 1

## In one sentence
The economy balance sheet says how much money players should earn at each stage of a 3-month season, where it comes from and where it goes, so prices, costs and rewards can be tuned together. The sheet and its model script exist and the owner approved them for the test server. Many of the systems it models (island upgrades, outposts, mastery, shop spawners) are not built yet.

## How it works
- **Two files.** `docs/ECONOMY_BALANCE.md` is the sheet. `tools/economy_model.py` (plain Python, no randomness) rebuilds every table in it. `--check` says if the sheet is out of date (today: up to date, 53 tables, the self-check passes), `--write` refreshes it.
- **The model reads the real game.** It takes numbers straight from the Java configs and bundled YAML (sell prices, skills, tools, enchants, wardrobe sets, auction fee, bank). The sheet's section 13 lists every key where the config differs from the model. Today there is one: `stacking.yml spawners.mobs-per-spawner` is 4 and the model needs 1.
- **Three player types, 900 players a day:** casual 1 h/day (540), regular 3 h/day (270), grinder 8 h/day (90).

- **Pacing (#61):** fast start (tier 2 within 3 played hours), then every tier takes longer. A regular's gaps between tiers are 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 11, 18, 40, 67 and 112 hours. Jumps come at each new tier, the Forge, spawner stack steps and the Coinmaster set. A grinder reaches tier 12 on day 51.
- **Where money comes from (regular, season):** The Depths about 50%, island about 33% (production 15%, spawners 18%), events and quests 15%, interest 2.8%. Interest stays at 8% or less for everyone (casual 7.7%, grinder 0.9%).
- **Where money goes (sinks):** Forge, sets, spawners, island level upgrades, late projects, an endless late sink (called Mastery in the sheet), bank tiers, shop blocks, AH fee and tax. There are no pure money burns.
- **Sink ratio:** the economy removes 85% of what it creates over the season (target 80% or more). The 14-day average dips to 57% for one day (day 30). "Act" means under 60% for 3 days in a row. Without the endless sink the season ratio falls to 38%.
- **Money supply:** about $1.7T held on day 90, $1.9B per player, about 12 days of a regular's income. Watch above 10 days, because that drives AH prices. Server prices are fixed numbers, so only the AH and `/trade` can inflate.
- **Sell bonuses** are added up and capped once at +300% (#46). A grinder ends the season at about +128%, so normal play never reaches the cap.
- **Planned (decided): no paid sell bonus in the model.** `tools/economy_model.py` still has a store rank bonus built in (`SELL_RANK_MAX = 0.15`, 4% regular, 8% grinder). Bought ranks give 0% sell and XP bonus, and gameplay perks are never sold (R3-7, REVIEW3 F7). Income is then about 4-8% lower. Rerun the model once F7 is locked.
- **Biggest risk: spawners.** At 4 mobs per spawn they become about two thirds of a regular's income (money supply $4.4T on day 90). If the shop price does not rise per owned spawner, supply reaches $12.9T. The sheet assumes 1 mob and a shop price +10% per owned spawner.
- **Planned (decided): the new spawner model (REVIEW3 F2, R3-4).** A fixed shop price with output that falls per island and tier: spawners 1-16 at 100%, 17-32 at 60%, 33-64 at 35%, 65-128 at 20%, 129+ at 10%. It replaces +10% per owned spawner (642). A patched copy of the model gives a sink ratio of 84% and supply of $1.8T on day 90, the same as the +10% plan with no trading; with alts buying at base the +10% plan falls to 75% and $3.7T. The repo sheet has not been re-run with F2. The numbers are fine-tuned in the D2 card.
- **Fix now (R3-21b):** the live `stacking.yml` still has `mobs-per-spawner: 4.0`. Set it to 1, as decided in 641. Until then spawners earn 4x what the sheet assumes.
- **Gems are now a gap.** Sheet section 9.2 says gems cannot cause inflation because nothing exchanges them for money. Bought gems are tradeable (REVIEW3 F3), so players can sell them for money. The model has no gem sales yet.
- **Day 3 (R3-37):** the grinder is at $12.9M against the owner's $5M (2.6x), the regular at $1.5M. Tuning down to $5M would make a 100x jump to week 2's $500M, so the curve stays and the target is re-checked on the test server.
- **Planned: Phase 10 in the sheet (M10.13):** crates, quests, votes and pass rewards stay under about 15% of a regular's income (crates about 5%). The sheet has no section for them yet.

## What the player sees
- Nothing directly. Players feel it as pace: about $40-70k in the first hour, and a new tier every few hours at first.
- Staff see the real numbers in the `/eco admin` dashboard (C10) and can compare them with this sheet.

## Rules and limits
- The sheet is a draft target, not a promise. After the first real week on the test server, the dashboard shows the truth and the sheet is tuned to it (650).
- Levers if the dashboard disagrees (sheet 7.4): sink ratio low means raise late sink prices; income over 2x the table means check dupes first; spawner share over 25% means lower mobs per spawner; interest over 8% means lower payout caps; AH prices running away means a higher AH tax or more late sinks.
- Any change to prices, costs, rewards or sell bonuses must be run through the model first, so the sink ratio stays at 80% or more.
- Rewards never print money. The AFK pool at its top step pays 5% or less of an active regular's income (O10).
